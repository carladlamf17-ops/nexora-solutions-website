AdSelect and the promise of getting paid to watch ads

An offer using a familiar name can come from the platform, a third party, or an impersonator. Check identity and terms before depositing.

What the sources document

This edition separates the platform’s statements from the FTC’s general task-scam warning. The FTC source describes the pattern and does not name AdSelect. We have not confirmed an official decision classifying it as a Ponzi scheme.

  • FTC · Estafas de tareas / Task scams

Fictional example

  • Example message from a supposed manager
  • “You have a pending balance.”
  • “Top up today and you can withdraw it.”

Side-by-side comparison

A promise in a chat

A name, screenshot, and testimonial leave two questions open: who operates the domain, and who receives the funds?

What you can verify

Independently check the identity and terms. Compare the exact domain and ask for payment and withdrawal rules you can keep.

What to do, step by step

  1. Avoid paying a supposed manager to access task earnings.
  2. Save the written domain, chat, and terms you were offered.
  3. If you paid, contact your payment provider and avoid anyone demanding a new deposit to recover funds.
