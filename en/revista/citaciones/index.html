The summons that turns into a demand for money

It starts with a complaint and may include a notice with seals. Then someone asks you to pay to avoid the proceedings.

Nexora SolutionsIssue 01November 9, 2026

First comes the supposed complaint. Then comes a file that looks like a summons. The final message in the reconstruction demands money to close the case. That is the moment to save the conversation and verify the document with the agency through a channel you find independently.

La Prensa de Monagas reported the CICPC director’s warning about fake forms used for extortion. The sample below is a text recreation without real seals, signatures, case files, or personal data. See sources

Read the text in the image
  • FICTIONAL NOTICE · Alleged summons
  • “There’s a complaint against you.”
  • “Pay today and we’ll close the case.”

Watch the news clip

“There’s a complaint against you” Simulated scenes

What the document shows

A copied signature, seal, or name can look official. The sender controls the numbers, links, and payment details in their own message.

How to verify

Find the agency’s contact independently or go to its office. Explain that you received a message and ask how to verify it.

Practical guide

What to do, step by step

  1. End the conversation if someone pressures you for money or personal data.
  2. Keep the original message, file, number, and time.
  3. Verify with the agency and submit the evidence through an institutional channel if someone tried to extort you.
