“Cousin, I got a new number”: how the fake dollar sale starts

The first message seeks recognition. The offer, urgency, and new payment recipient appear once you trust the conversation.

Nexora SolutionsIssue 01November 9, 2026

“Cousin, I got a new number.” In the simulated chat, the greeting comes before any mention of money. Then comes an offer to sell dollars and, when it is time to pay, the money is supposed to go to someone else. That change of recipient is the moment to leave the chat and verify who is behind it.

Venevisión reported the CICPC warning. WhatsApp provides account recovery instructions. The conversation below illustrates the pattern and comes from no actual complaint. See sources

Simulated example

Read the text in the image
  • “Cousin, I got a new number.”
  • “I have cheap dollars.”
  • “Send the Pago Móvil payment to this other person and I’ll set them aside.”

“Cousin, I got a new number” Simulated scenes

What the chat shows

A photo, voice note, or familiar number can build trust. Someone may also have taken over the real account.

What you should confirm

Call the number you already saved outside that chat or ask in person. Confirm who is selling, the amount, and who will receive payment.

Practical guide

What to do, step by step

  1. Pause payment if you cannot confirm the offer independently.
  2. If it was impersonation, alert your relative through another channel and save the chat and recipient details.
  3. If you already paid, contact your bank through its official channel and keep the reference handy.
