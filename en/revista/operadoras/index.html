“We’ll cut off your line”: what the fake technician is after

A threat to suspend service or an upgrade announcement can disguise a request for codes or money. The SMS often provides the most useful clue.

Nexora SolutionsIssue 01November 9, 2026

In the simulated call, the supposed technician promises a 5G upgrade. The message that arrives on the phone describes something else: registering WhatsApp. Reading the whole SMS before sharing the digits reveals what the code is for.

Movistar Venezuela documented calls promising an alleged 5G upgrade and asking for a WhatsApp-related code. The linked carrier notice is the source for this case. See sources

Simulated example

Read the text in the image
  • Call: “Read me the code you just received to upgrade to 5G.”
  • Simulated SMS: “WhatsApp registration code. Do not share it.”

What the caller claims

The caller claims authority and rushes you. Knowing your name, carrier, or phone number gives little evidence of their identity.

What the SMS says

If the message identifies the code as a WhatsApp registration code, giving it away can let someone try to register the account. Verify your plan in your carrier’s app.

Practical guide

What to do, step by step

  1. Hang up before sharing a code, opening a link, or scanning a QR code.
  2. Check your balance and plan through the official app or website. If you owe money, use the channel you verified.
  3. If you shared the code and lost WhatsApp access, use its official recovery guide.
