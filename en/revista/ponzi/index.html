Your balance goes up. Where does the money come from?

A dashboard with green numbers and a small withdrawal can persuade you to put in more. What matters is who funds those payments.

Nexora SolutionsIssue 01November 9, 2026

In the example, you deposit a hundred dollars and the screen shows two hundred. You withdraw ten and the system seems to work. Then you try to withdraw the rest and a new condition appears: top up. The first withdrawal was a payment. Finding out whether there was a profit means asking where that money came from.

Investor.gov explains how Ponzi schemes pay earlier participants with new contributions. The FTC documents a different pattern, task scams that display fictional commissions and demand deposits to cash out. See sources

Read the text in the image
  • First deposit: USD 100
  • Displayed balance: USD 200
  • First withdrawal: USD 10
  • New message: “Top up to unlock the balance.”

Watch the news clip

“Double your money” Simulated scenes

An early payout

It shows money was sent. The source, business earnings, and ability to withdraw the remainder all need separate evidence.

A verifiable offer

Independently check the operator’s legal identity and authorization where required. Ask how the business works and get the withdrawal terms in writing.

Practical guide

What to do, step by step

  1. Identify the payment recipient and check whether it matches the operator.
  2. Ask what generates the returns. Investigate claims of fixed returns or recruitment bonuses.
  3. If they demand a top-up or pressure you to deposit today, stop paying and save the materials.
